So... I've been observing performant JavaScript practices in use for most of my career. Given that I never studied Computer Science, I had no scientific basis for what was good or bad (if I had taken Computer Science at the time that I went to college... they would have taught me COBOL and FORTRAN). This course covers the fundamentals of execution context, memory, and task queueing to explain how JavaScript functions and interacts with things external to it (like the browser).

I think everyone who writes JavaScript should take this course.
